Joint Tensor Rank Constraints Can Shorten
Honest Functional Confidence Intervals
Research working draft
September 9, 2026
Status.The six numbered theorems have complete derivations in the appendix and supplementary
numerical checks. This is a working research note, without independent proof review or a claim of
certified priority. Sharp minimax length, general loading geometry and adaptation to the actual
rank remain open. No conjecture is stated as a theorem.
Abstract
We study confidence intervals for a prescribed linear functional of a fully observed Gaussian
tensor mean, under a known Frobenius bound and upper bounds on its Tucker or tensor-train
ranks. Actual ranks may be smaller than the supplied caps, and nonzero singular values may
be arbitrarily small. For an orthogonal tensor contrast, canonical decompositions imply an
ℓ2/K bound on the relevant diagonal, whereK is the tensor order. Classical soft thresholding
then gives an explicit finite-sample honest interval. For equal mode sizep, fixed rank caps,
normalized diagonal loading and norm boundM =τ√p, its length isO{τ (logp/p )(K−2)/(2K)},
where τ is the coordinate noise standard deviation. WhenK≥ 3, this is smaller in order
than every fixed-radius affine interval on the same class, and than the expected length at zero
of every interval required to cover the larger class defined by one unfolding constraint. A
within-class lower bound leaves a polynomial minimax gap. In 172 Gaussian configurations with
2,000 replications each, the selected interval has empirical coverage between 99.75% and 100%,
indicating substantial conservatism. The results establish two specific length comparisons; they
do not establish unrestricted optimality or adaptation to the actual rank.
Keywords:honest confidence intervals; linear functionals; Gaussian sequence model; tensor train;
Tucker rank; nonconvex parameter space.
1 Introduction
Low-rank tensor models impose several rank restrictions simultaneously. Whether these restrictions
help estimation is different from whether they permit shorter confidence intervals that remain
valid when components vanish. Consider a third-orderp×p×p tensor meanA, observed through
n =p independent full tensors with independent standard Gaussian errors. Its sample mean has
coordinate noise levelτ = p−1/2. Suppose∥A∥F≤ 1and the tensor has rank at most one. Our
running estimand is the normalized diagonal sump−1/2∑p
i=1Aiii. The direct Gaussian interval
is valid regardless of rank. The question is whether the tensor restriction permits a substantially
shorter interval while retaining coverage at every admissible signal, including zero.
Several parts of this question have established precedents. Tensor functional inference on regular
low-rank strata is developed by Xu et al. [2025]; matrix inference without estimating the exact
rank is available under quantitative conditions [Choi et al., 2026]. Honest adaptation and nonlinear
functional estimation on nonconvex classes have a much older theory [Cai and Low, 2004a,b].
1

Consequently, neither unknown rank nor nonlinear inference is a sufficient novelty claim. A more
specific question is whether the simultaneous tensor restrictions produce an inferential advantage
that disappears when the honesty class retains only one unfolding rank constraint.
We answer this restricted comparison for a class of prescribed orthogonal loadings. The
observation model is a Gaussian location experiment with known noise variance, and the norm and
rank caps are supplied. We require finite-sample coverage over the entire cap class, with no lower
bound on any nonzero singular value. For fixed orderK≥ 3, equal mode sizep and M =τ√p, we
construct an interval of lengtho(τ). We then show that an honest fixed-radius affine interval on the
same class must have length of order at leastτ, and that every interval honest over a single-unfolding
relaxation must have expected length of order at leastτ at zero. Matricization itself preserves all
the data; the comparison concerns enlarging the parameter space.
Three issues govern the argument. First, a bound valid at rank boundaries must avoid inversion
of small singular values. Second, a comparison with the relaxed model must cover all interval
procedures on that model, rather than one chosen estimator. Third, a constructive upper bound
must be distinguished from a sharp minimax result. The last distinction matters here: the lower
bound inside the joint tensor class does not match the thresholding upper bound.
Canonical Tucker and tensor-train expansions provide a controlled sum of squared component
weights. Applied to an orthogonal diagonal, this yields anℓ2/K envelope. The exponent is familiar
from tensor factor quasi-norm geometry [Fan et al., 2023]; the estimator uses classical thresholding
[Donoho and Johnstone, 1994]. A simultaneous Gaussian noise bound converts that envelope into an
honest scalar interval. Gaussian mixtures with a common functional value supply the relaxed-class
and within-class lower bounds. In the running third-order example, the resulting radius is at most
τ{8 log(2p/α)/p}1/6, despite the absence of an identifiable nonzero factor at the origin.
The note makes three concrete contributions to this restricted formulation:
1. An explicit diagonal bound for Tucker and TT cap classes, and a finite-sample interval valid
across their lower-rank strata (Sections 4–5).
2. Two quantitative separations: from fixed-radius affine intervals under the same honesty class,
and from all intervals under a one-unfolding relaxation (Section 5).
3. A within-class lower bracket and reproducible experiments that expose the remaining lack of
sharpness and actual-rank adaptation (Sections 5.3–6).
The precise separation appears distinct from the inspected literature, but its elementary ingredients
make a reduction to existing general theory a serious remaining novelty question.
2 Related work
Functional inference and nonconvex classes.Optimal recovery connects confidence and
estimation problems to parameter-set geometry [Donoho, 1994]. Nonlinear improvements on
nonconvex classes and the limits of honest adaptation are established themes [Cai and Low, 2004b,a,
Low, 1997]. Finite unions of convex classes admit general aggregation and functional-inference
constructions [Juditsky and Nemirovski, 2020]. These results supply both tools and a substantial
novelty threat: the thresholding step below is not a new general inference principle. The particular
comparison requires an upper bound exploiting joint tensor restrictions and a lower bound over the
enlarged unfolding class.
Matrix and tensor uncertainty quantification.Low-rank matrix linear forms, geometric
inverse problems and rank-cap confidence sets already have extensive theory [Xia and Yuan, 2021,
Cai et al., 2016, Carpentier and Kim, 2018, Carpentier et al., 2018]. Tensor inference includes
2

subspace and functional results in strong-signal regimes [Xia et al., 2022, Agterberg and Zhang,
2024], as well as uncertainty quantification for tensor completion [Cai et al., 2023]. Our fully
observed experiment is simpler than completion or random-design regression. Our loading is also
narrower than an arbitrary functional. What changes is the uniformity class: every tensor below the
supplied caps is covered, without a minimum-signal condition. The resulting interval is not asserted
to retain regular-stratum efficiency.
Weak factors and singularity.Weak-factor inference, rank uncertainty and adaptation obstruc-
tions are studied in econometrics [Zhu, 2019, Armstrong et al., 2026, Choi et al., 2026]; uniform
inference near critical matrix signal strengths is also under active development [Bykhovskaya et al.,
2025]. Singular likelihood asymptotics [Drton, 2009] and universal inference [Wasserman et al.,
2020] further show why validity at a singularity is not itself a new objective. Here the target is a
tensor contrast, and the claim is a specific length separation.
Tensor geometry.Orthonormal Tucker representations and TT canonicalization are standard
[De Lathauwer et al., 2000, Oseledets, 2011]. Tensor norm geometry and factor penalties are
developed by Friedland and Lim [2018], Fan et al. [2023], while rank-variety tangent cones are
studied by Gao et al. [2025], Kutschan [2018]. Our proof uses canonical expansions rather than
estimating tangent spaces. The explicit rank constants below are sufficient bounds; their optimality
is unknown. A companion literature dossier records 20 closest competitors, theorem assumptions
and source-inspection depth, with a bibliography covering relevant work available by September 9,
2026.

for dependent LLM representations.

6 Simulation study
6.1 Protocol and baselines
The simulation protocol was written after the theorem derivations and before implementation. It
uses seed 20260909, 2,000 replications per configuration, and exact marginal observations of the
diagonal coordinates needed by the procedures. Unused independent Gaussian coordinates of the
full tensor need not be generated. This does not simulate a regression design or a tensor completion
experiment.
The main grid takesK∈{ 2, 3, 4, 6}, p =n∈{ 128, 512, 2048, 8192}, M =σ = 1, and rank cap
one. Signals have the formA = au⊗K, witha∈{ 0, 0.02, 0.2, 1} and u uniform on a coordinate
support. For nonzero signals, support sizes include one,p, and a value near(a/λ)2/K, with duplicates
removed. The latter places diagonal means near the threshold. Additional configurations varyn/p
and the valid threshold multiplier, and use cap two with a second component tending to zero. There
are 175 configurations, of which three deliberately violate the iid Gaussian model.
We compareIG, I0, INL and C∗. For nonzero rank-one signals, a known-direction oracle is also
included. IfU =u⊗K were supplied, thenc⟨U,Z⟩±zτ|c| , wherec =⟨L,U⟩ , is exact forA =aU. Its
joint Gaussian noise with the diagonal is simulated exactly. This oracle has unavailable structural
information and a smaller honesty class; it is not an implementation of an estimated-subspace
method. Published strong-signal methods are compared through their assumptions rather than
advertised as uniform baselines without a matching implementation.
Each output row reports coverage, binomial Monte Carlo standard error, an exact 95% Clopper–
Pearson interval, deterministic length, center bias and its standard error, and root mean squared
center error. Width standard errors are zero because the radii are deterministic. Numerical proof
checks separately examine 101,101 scalar soft-threshold cases, 120 random Tucker and 120 random
TT tensors, and exact finite-binomial mixture second moments. They supplement rather than
replace the proofs.

7 Practical relevance and limits of an AI application
The theory describes repeated, calibrated tensor measurements and a predetermined orthogonal
contrast. A scientifically justified application would need a sampling model supporting the noise
envelope, a credible outer norm/rank class, and a loading fixed independently of the observations. We
do not supply a real-data experiment because no such dataset and calibration have been established
in this project.
In particular, indexing an array by model, layer and prompt does not make iid Gaussian tensor
location an adequate model for LLM representations. Repeated prompts, dependent tokens, layer
coupling, heterogeneous measurement noise and data-selected contrasts materially change the
experiment. An LLM example would currently add application terminology without additional
statistical theory. A separate dependence and calibration analysis is required before such an
experiment could substantiate this note.
8 Discussion
The central result is a concrete comparison of honesty classes. Under a special loading and bounded
tensor ranks, a nonlinear interval can be smaller in order than every fixed-radius affine interval
on the same class, and than every interval honest on a one-unfolding relaxation. The underlying
tensor data need not be compressed or factor-estimated to obtain this comparison. Conversely, an
algorithm using a matricized tensor can still exploit all original constraints; Theorem 4 does not
rule out such an algorithm.
The current upper bound is not minimax sharp. The polynomial gap in(22) is the most
important theoretical limitation, followed by the restricted loading, loose growing-rank constants,
known norm and noise bounds, and lack of actual-rank adaptation. The experiments also show
that the interval is conservative on the tested Gaussian grid. Strong-signal procedures or additional
known-direction information may provide substantially better pointwise precision.
The most valuable next theorem would determineL0 orLmax for the rank-one class, or character-
ize which loadings admit a joint-constraint advantage. A rank-adaptive procedure would additionally
10

need honest calibration over the larger cap class and a theorem identifying the spectra or geometries
on which shorter intervals are possible. A naive plug-in rank or variance estimate would not resolve
these questions.
This draft is therefore a constructive research note and a basis for further theory, rather than a
completed general solution to honest inference across unknown tensor ranks. The explicit separation
was not found in the inspected literature, but the proof combines familiar ingredients. Its suitability
for a strong statistics journal depends on resolving a substantial part of the remaining theory and
confirming that the tensor specialization is not a direct consequence already established elsewhere.
Reproducibility.The accompanying project contains the prespecified simulation protocol, modu-
lar Python code, fixed seeds, 817 result rows, exact binomial uncertainty, source and result hashes,

B Result status and unresolved claims
Theorem 1 (diagonal envelope), Theorem 2 (honest interval), Theorem 3 (affine radius), Theorem 4
(relaxed-class lower bound), Theorem 5 (two separations), and Theorem 6 (within-class lower bound)
are labeledPROVEDin the project ledger on the basis of these derivations. None asserts a
previously unknown general inference principle.
Sharp minimax length and actual-rank/spectrum adaptation arePARTIAL: the current
upper/lower bracket and valid cap-based procedure establish components but do not solve either
target. The possibility of attaining the coordinate-mixture lower scale at zero using off-diagonal
information isCONJECTURED; a more difficult mixture could invalidate that conjecture. General
loadings, unknown norm/variance bounds, dependence, contamination and an LLM application
require additional formulation and theory. They are not results of this manuscript.
