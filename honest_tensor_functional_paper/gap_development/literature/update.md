# Literature check for the gap development

Searches included sparse harmonic rank-one tensor mixtures; tensor functional minimax Gaussian means; full-data tensor confidence interval diameter; sparse tensor PCA computational lower bounds; low-degree tensor estimation; and signed-permutation moment bounds. The sparse harmonic support construction made the last line unnecessary.

No equivalent sharp-rate statement for the prescribed equal diagonal contrast on the full Tucker/TT cap class was identified in the sources inspected. This does not establish priority.

## Closest mathematical connections

- [Collier, Comminges and Tsybakov (2017)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.00665): the Gaussian support-mixture and factorial-moment mechanism is classical. The new cubic argument changes the tensor profile so that its full inner product is bounded by a constant times support overlap. It does not transfer a diagonal likelihood calculation to full data.
- [Cai and Low (2004)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503662): confidence-set projection and expected-length lower bounds belong to established testing/modulus methodology. G2 evaluates a tensor-specific diameter; it does not propose a new generic confidence-set inversion principle.
- [Choo and d'Orsi (2021), The Complexity of Sparse Tensor PCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.06308): full-text Theorem 26 (pp. 17–18 of the 34-page version) and its Hermite/overlap proof structure were inspected. It gives a more detailed low-degree detection analysis for sparse Rademacher factors. Our nonnegative-prior overlap bound is deliberately coarse and proved independently; the scalar-functional estimation consequence is the relevant comparison.
- [Fu and Sohn (2026), Low-degree estimation thresholds in planted hypergraphs and tensor PCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30113): the full text was inspected for its model definitions and main estimation/recovery scope. It studies sharper low-degree thresholds and efficient algorithms in specified planted regimes. Those latent-coordinate/recovery results are not automatically lower bounds for every scalar confidence interval. No theorem from that paper is an unverified proof dependency.

## What is and is not new

The strongest apparent contribution is the evaluated full-data minimax rate, including the cubic logarithmic distinction, together with a restricted estimation obstruction for the same scalar target. Soft thresholding, support mixtures, confidence-set projection, Hermite expansions and the low-degree framework are prior methodology.

The manuscript does not claim a new general sparse tensor PCA threshold, a complete computational-statistical phase diagram, an efficient minimax confidence interval, or a reduction from planted clique. Independent mathematical-priority review remains warranted.
