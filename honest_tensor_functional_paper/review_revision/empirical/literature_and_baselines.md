# Baseline selection and primary-source verification

Checked 2026-09-12. This is a focused empirical-method comparison, not an exhaustive novelty review.

- The classical diagonal-functional comparator follows the Gaussian sequence setting in Collier, Comminges and Tsybakov, *Minimax estimation of linear and quadratic functionals on sparsity classes*, [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00665), especially the linear-functional results. The repository's existing soft-threshold interval adds its explicit simultaneous-noise/Frobenius calibration; it is not advertised as the exact optimal estimator for every sequence regime. The direct Gaussian interval and deterministic structural interval are necessary strong baselines, since each is optimal in an available regime.
- Xu, Chen and Han, *Statistical Inference for Low-Rank Tensor Models*, [primary preprint, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16223v1), Section 4.2, Section 4.4 and Algorithm 3, provides the closest tensor-functional computational comparator. The local reference PDF was inspected on pp. 17–19 and in Appendix N.2. We implement HOSVD initialization, two simultaneous power iterations, tensor projection, the fitted-core row-space variance formula and residual noise estimation. The known-noise variant shares our supplied tau; the plug-in variant follows their variance estimate. This is a checked local reimplementation, not a claim to run official author software. Its asymptotic strong-signal guarantees do not imply uniform weak-signal coverage.
- The earlier repository separated-rank interval is retained as a precisely scoped comparison: its rank selector needs a spectral gap. It is deliberately stress-tested outside that class, with those rows labeled accordingly.
- The oracle uses known support or a known complete rank-one direction. It receives unavailable structure and covers a smaller parameter class; it quantifies an idealized precision reference rather than a deployable competitor.

The exact Tucker tangent variance differs at ranks above one from the larger first-order product-space projection used for our cap-valid bias certificate. The implementation tests compare the competitor's variance with a separately constructed tangent Jacobian basis at ranks 1, 2 and 3, and test noiseless reconstruction. Cached cap-valid calculations are checked against the preserved original implementation at 36 combinations of observation, cap and confidence level.

No tensor-recovery benchmark is used as a substitute for scalar inference. A broad contest against every tensor algorithm would not address the fixed-loading coverage/rate question, and no broad superiority claim is made.
