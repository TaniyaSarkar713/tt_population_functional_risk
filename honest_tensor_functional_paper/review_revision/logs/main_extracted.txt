Honest Tensor-Functional Inference:
Full-Data Minimax Rates and Computational Limits
Revised September 13, 2026
Abstract
We study honest confidence intervals for a prescribed linear functional of a Gaussian tensor
mean under supplied Tucker or tensor-train rank caps and a Frobenius norm bound. For a
normalized flat orthogonal loading and fixed positive caps, we characterize full-data minimax
expected length at zero and in the worst case for every positive norm and noise budget, together
with the corresponding squared-risk rate. At M = τ√p, the length scale is τ{log(ep)}3/2/√p
for order three and τ
p
log(ep)/p for higher orders. Residual scans and full-likelihood mixtures
explain the distinction from the diagonal observation experiment. We also delimit matched
growing-cap and loading regimes, adaptation obstructions, and efficient strong saturated pointwise
inference. A separate scalar companion gives uniform noise-scale risk without changing the
intervals. Controlled simulations examine supplied-budget sensitivity, component ablations,
scale calibration and computational gaps. General efficient fixed-cap weak-signal computation,
arbitrary-loading rates and complete pointwise adaptation remain open.
Keywords: honest confidence intervals; nonconvex parameter spaces; tensor mean; Tucker rank;
tensor train; minimax inference.
1
Introduction
A tensor rank bound restricts several unfoldings of the same mean. For inference, the useful question
is how much these simultaneous restrictions shorten a confidence interval when the actual rank
is unknown. Consider a cubic p × p × p tensor A, a supplied rank cap one, and the normalized
diagonal contrast ψ(A) = p−1/2 P
i Aiii. Observing Z = A + τG gives a direct Gaussian interval of
length proportional to τ. An interval honest under the cap must cover at zero, at every smaller-rank
mean, and along signals too weak to recover a factor reliably. Can the full tensor still yield a shorter
interval?
Classical nonconvex functional inference supplies general geometric and testing tools [Cai and
Low, 2004b,a], and sparse Gaussian sequence theory gives sharp rates for diagonal observations
[Collier et al., 2017]. Tensor functional inference on regular strata instead uses subspace estimation,
debiasing and tangent projections under quantitative signal and remainder conditions [Xu et al.,
2025]. These results concern different experiments or honesty requirements. In particular, a lower
bound for the diagonal marginal does not establish a lower bound for procedures using every tensor
entry.
We study the full Gaussian location experiment with a prescribed loading, known noise scale,
supplied Frobenius budget and supplied Tucker or tensor-train (TT) rank caps. Our central goal
is to determine honest expected length at zero and in the worst case for every positive norm
budget. The corresponding squared-error problem requires a separate risk argument. The target is

a linear functional of the tensor itself; nonuniqueness of its factor coordinates does not create an

Three difficulties determine the analysis. First, uniform geometry at rank boundaries cannot
divide by an unknown minimum singular value. Second, indistinguishability must be established
under the full tensor likelihood, including off-diagonal entries.
Third, the norm budget must
remain inside the ordered-coordinate argument to recover the intermediate cubic regime. Statistical
existence, computational implementation and pointwise adaptation then require distinct conclusions.
Canonical tensor expansions yield a diagonal compressibility envelope. Finite residual scans
use the remaining tensor entries to constrain ordered diagonal discrepancies. A sparse harmonic
mixture matches the cubic logarithmic multiplier under the full likelihood; for higher orders the
relevant series is summable. Integrating residual excess tails gives the squared-risk bound uniformly
over the norm budget. This analysis identifies a difference between the tensor experiment and its
classical sequence marginal, while crediting the latter as an essential benchmark.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we give sharp all-budget length and squared-risk rates
for flat orthogonal loadings under fixed positive Tucker or TT caps, including weak signals and
zero (Theorem 1). Second, we identify matched growing-cap and decaying-loading regimes and
distinguish these from unresolved arbitrary profiles and general efficient computation (Section 4).
Third, we connect an executable cap-valid interval to a strong saturated pointwise theorem, add a
separately proved scalar-risk companion, and test the actual assumptions and numerical limitations
in controlled experiments (Sections 5–7). The general weak-signal minimax optimizer remains
an ideal statistical construction; the implemented exponential search is a restricted binary-model
benchmark.

3.3
Comparison with neighboring inference problems
At M = τ√p, an honest fixed-radius affine interval on the same rank-one class has radius at least
a constant times τ. An arbitrary interval required to be honest on a single-unfolding relaxation
likewise has expected length at zero at least order τ (Supplement Theorems 3 and 4). Matricization
retains the data; the second comparison enlarges the parameter class over which coverage is required.
Neither assertion rules out regular-stratum pointwise gains.
The novelty claim is therefore specific: an ordered residual argument and a full-likelihood
harmonic mixture yield the cubic all-budget phase diagram under simultaneous tensor constraints.
General nonlinear inference on nonconvex sets and honest-adaptation obstructions are established
subjects [Cai and Low, 2004b,a, Juditsky and Nemirovski, 2020]. The geometric exponent and the
sparse marginal benchmark are also precedents, not separate claims of new general principles.
4
Computation, growing caps and loading geometry
4.1
What is implemented
The residual projection and minimum-residual estimator are statistical constructions. Finite nets do
not make the optimization efficient: the number of support/direction constraints can be exponential,
and fitting the nonconvex cap class is a second obstacle. A certified residual fit must return a feasible
tensor with objective excess at most cτ on every observation to inherit the stated squared-risk
order. A bound only on successful runs is insufficient. For confidence projection, conservative outer
endpoints preserve validity even when the requested endpoint tolerance is not attained; achieved
precision must be reported separately. Supplement C1 gives the exact optimization contracts.

Adaptation and an executable pointwise result
Positive fixed outer caps allow rate adaptation to smaller positive fixed caps because Theorem 1
has the same order, with constants allowed to depend on the outer cap. This assertion is neither
rank recovery nor uniform adaptation as the outer caps grow. At M = τ√p and outer minimum
cap r ≳√p, every outer-honest interval has expected length at zero at least order τ. The known
fixed-cap oracle rate is o(τ). Since zero is in every positive inner cap class, simultaneous minimax
adaptation across these classes is impossible (Supplement G2). A translated disjoint-block mixture
gives a related obstruction at nonzero means when extra weak rank fits within the caps and norm
budget.
A different conclusion holds at strong means saturating the supplied Tucker mode caps. The
following result does not substitute TT bond caps for Tucker mode caps.

Noise, supplied bounds and calibration
The baseline noise model has separate upper- and lower-bound roles. Joint sub-Gaussian directional
control permits the residual-scan upper bounds with a known proxy. Marginal coordinate bounds
alone do not calibrate all product directions. Gaussian likelihood identities give the lower bounds; a
minimax lower bound over a noise family containing Gaussians does not prove an identical lower
bound for each fixed non-Gaussian law.
With independent full replicates and a known covariance upper bound, directional block medians
yield finite-variance scan bounds when the number of independent replicates exceeds the required
scan complexity. Replicate independence is needed for concentration across blocks. Arbitrary
dependence cannot be repaired by interpreting the total number of recorded tensors as independent
sample size. A simpler coordinate refinement allows a known number c of corrupted whole replicates:
choose an odd number of blocks b > max{8c, 32 log(m/α)}, require n ≥2b, and use threshold
Λ = 2¯σ/
p
⌊n/b⌋. Median contraction errors are then simultaneously bounded with the stated
probability (Supplement Proposition 13). This is a coordinate-envelope calibration, not a sharp
general contamination minimax theorem.
Unknown scale has a finite-sample solution in the independent Gaussian replicate setting. With
m orthogonal contractions, n ≥2 and residual variance S2, the degrees of freedom are ν = m(n −1).

A supplied Frobenius approximation allowance εA adds its size, up to the direct-noise ceiling,
to the flat-loading interval scale; an uncharged loading residual can similarly invalidate coverage.
Data-selected loadings are outside the prescribed-loading theory unless selection is made independent
or calibrated uniformly. Missing-entry experiments, dependent replicates without a concentration
model and fully unknown directional scale remain separate problems. The supplement retains the
complete assumptions and counterexamples for these distinctions.

Calibration and assumption violations.
All 18 within-cap whole-replicate corruption cases
have median-interval coverage 1.000 in 1,000 trials each, with conservative lengths; the deliberately
violated 120-row/zero-supplied-cap case has coverage 0.000. For m = 1, n = 2, σ = 1, independent
upper-scale calibration has coverage 0.995 versus 0.703 for the naive plug-in. Gaussian residual
independence and the chi-square degrees of freedom are part of the experiment, not assumed for
arbitrary noise. Fourth-moment-based MSE error bars are not asserted for the heavy-tail cases.
Optimization and input cost.
V02 fixes the arbitrary loading, signal direction, norm budget,
noise and tolerance across all budgets. Of 600 solves, 7 meet endpoint tolerance 0.05, all at the
largest budget and strongest amplitude; 593 remain unresolved. Mean gaps at 3,000 nodes range
from 0.127 to 0.353. All feasible-witness gaps are finite and retained, including failures. These
guarded float64 outer bounds do not implement the ideal high-dimensional minimax estimator.
Fresh timings of the revised public scalar/interval interface use one thread, one warmup and ten
calls per dimension; p = 64 requires 2,097,152 input bytes and averages 0.0876 seconds per call.
Traced allocations exclude the preallocated input and potentially untraced native workspace.
The XCH comparison is a local implementation of the tensor-PCA procedure in Section 4.2 of
Xu et al. [2025]: HOSVD initialization and two simultaneous projection updates, with known-noise
and residual plug-in scales. Its regular-stratum theorem requires more than correct rank. Zero
and rank-mismatched cases are explicitly outside the specified model; initialization, alignment and
remainder constants are not certified for the other individual cases. No inference of finite-sample
cap-uniform coverage or leading-constant dominance is made from these comparisons.
8
Discussion and remaining problems
The full tensor contains useful functional information beyond its diagonal marginal even when
the class permits arbitrarily weak signals. For flat orthogonal loadings and fixed positive caps,
the residual geometry and full-likelihood mixtures determine all-budget minimax rates. The cubic
11


[PAGE 12]
logarithmic transition is the main tensor-specific statistical conclusion. The rank-one image identity,
soft-thresholding benchmark and generic adaptation logic have classical antecedents and are not
presented as new general principles.
Four substantial extensions remain. First, no general efficient optimizer is implemented or proved
minimax for the original fixed-cap diffuse-loading weak-signal class. The binary search illustrates
a rigorous real-arithmetic contract with numerical bounds, not a scalable resolution.
Second,
intermediate growing caps can leave a substantial gap between (5) and available upper bounds.
Third, arbitrary loading profiles beyond the matched flat, fixed-decay and small-budget regimes
lack a complete sharp theory. Fourth, P1 covers strong saturated coordinate Tucker means with a
norm-comparable outer class; complete pointwise geometry, optimal signal thresholds, nonsaturated
means and TT-specific pointwise results are open. General growing-cap adaptation is already
impossible in the stated G2 comparison, so a universal positive adaptation theorem would be false.
The new scalar companion supplies noise-scale risk control with unchanged intervals.
Its
simplicity should not be mistaken for pointwise minimax squared risk. The simulations reveal
conservative constants, sensitivity to supplied budgets, weak-signal failure of out-of-domain regular
comparators, and unresolved optimization gaps. Full-tensor SVD computation costs O(KpK+1)
arithmetic with O(pK + p2) working storage at fixed order; diagonal coordinate access and dense
rotated contraction costs must be reported separately.
This is a theory and simulation paper.
No repeated-tensor application with a defensible
independent sampling unit and prescribed contrast was established in the available project materials.
A real-data study should identify that unit, dependence structure, noise calibration and norm/cap
justification before attaching practical interpretation to these intervals. Neither empirical success
on simulated cases nor a broader robustness paragraph substitutes for those requirements.
