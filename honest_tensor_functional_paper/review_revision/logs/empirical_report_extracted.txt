Revised empirical analysis
September 13, 2026. Canonical report accompanying the revised main paper and technical supplement.
Data and dependency accounting
The original study contains 1,084,640 saved datasets in 318 stochastic configurations plus 84 analytic-only con-
figurations. All 303 interval-producing cases were re-evaluated from their original raw draws with the four-ULP
policy and R1 scalar estimator. The remaining 15 event-only cases have no affected intervals. This is deterministic
method reevaluation, not 1,084,640 new draws. All original files are preserved. The revision adds 87,200 new
independent datasets, 55 configurations, and 600 binary optimizer solves on 200 observations. Three confidence
levels reuse observations, and their losses reuse an explicitly identified 95% scalar estimate where relevant; they are
not independent datasets.
Case-local seeds use master seed 2026091309, SHA-256 case identifiers, trial and stream. Original saved experiments
retain seed 2026091203. Configurations were registered before execution, with no outcome tuning. One thread was
used within each independent simulation process; paired procedures share data and pilot randomness. Fixed-M and
norm-comparable-M branches are labeled separately. Raw new outputs, the exactly derived pure-fallback ablation,
and independently recomputed summaries are kept separately.
Coverage and scalar corrections
Two corrected 90% rows, both S03_007 (known-noise XCH and support oracle), change from 134/150 to 133/150.
They correspond to one genuine Gaussian miss, not two independent failures.
Strict coverage and four-ULP
coverage are both retained; 24 strict/tolerant discrepancies at exact structural boundaries were previously verified
as harmless algebraic rounding. A target-proportional tolerance is no longer used.
The S03_002 midpoint MSE changes from 12,084,661.378502 to 0.147776 for the R1 estimator, on exactly the same
500 observations. Its MSE standard error is 0.024743, and its proved noise-scale bound is 9.600647. One original
sentinel event is retained, along with all old losses. All 303 cases have unchanged interval endpoints. The R1 bound
is marked inapplicable when the supplied cap or structural range is violated.

Findings and interpretation
Budget and ablation.
At amplitude two the full fixed-M interval covers 0.960; XCH covers 0.001, outside
certified regularity conditions. Bias-only removal has no effect over the first eight weak/intermediate signal levels
because the original fallback is selected. It cannot explain the combined diagnostic failure. Pure fallback removal,
which preserves the intersections, is conservative and longer in those cases. Changing only the validation quantile
reduces coverage at several levels. These comparisons isolate different mechanisms; they do not establish that every
correction is necessary at every mean.
Pointwise behavior. The four new larger cases have coverage 0.965–0.978 and normalized lengths 7.20–8.01.
Amplitudes deliberately satisfy the conservative P1 suﬀicient bound. Rounded caps and finite dimensions prevent

fitting an exact asymptotic exponent from these four points. Earlier slopes and their caveats remain in the corrected
archive; no favorable pooling across different regimes is performed.
Robustness. Median coverage is 1.000 for every tested within-cap row-contamination case, but interval lengths
are conservative. The 120-row/zero-supplied-cap violation has coverage 0.000. The uncorrected mean also fails
with correlated coordinates or row corruption. These tests address a coordinate-envelope proposition, not general
tensor contamination minimaxity.
Unknown variance.
The independent upper bound is conservative across the tested grid.
For m=1, n=2,
sigma=1, coverage is 0.9950 versus 0.7033 for the naive plug-in. At larger m, structural intervals often dominate;
coverage 1 there does not demonstrate accurate variance estimation or eﬀicient interval length. Calibration-event
probabilities and Wilson intervals are separately reported.
Computation. 7/600 binary solves meet the common tolerance; 593 remain unresolved. All seven successes are at
amplitude 2.5 and budget 3,000. Mean endpoint gaps at that budget range from 0.1275 to 0.3526. All observations
and finite witness gaps remain in coverage, width and gap summaries. This numerical search does not settle general
weak-signal computation.
Comparator and uncertainty contracts
XCH is the published tensor-PCA algorithm locally reimplemented from Xu–Chen–Han, Section 4.2, Algorithm
3; the related inference statements are in Section 4.3.
HOSVD and two simultaneous updates use the same
full observation as the other methods. Known-noise and plug-in variants are both retained. Zero and supplied-
rank mismatch cases are outside the specified regular class. Other case-level initialization, loading-alignment and
remainder constants are not certified, even if the signal is very large. The support oracle receives true support and
is labeled as an information benchmark.
Coverage error bars are Wilson 95% intervals; coverage MCSE and paired coverage-difference SE are also exported.
Mean-length and finite-moment loss summaries retain sample standard errors. No normal-theory MSE uncertainty
is claimed for the t3 experiment: sample diagnostics are retained separately because fourth-moment arguments
for the naive mean fail. Empty intervals, failures and catastrophic losses are never removed. No claim of uniform
validity is inferred solely from simulated coverage.
