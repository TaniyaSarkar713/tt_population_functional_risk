Technical Supplement: Honest Tensor-Functional Inference
Revised September 13, 2026
This supplement contains the complete supplied proofs supporting the accompanying revised main
paper. The main paper states the final central results and empirical findings; this document records
the auxiliary comparisons, calibrations, computational contracts and proofs. Fixed-budget intermediate
results are retained here as proof ingredients, not as unresolved final rate claims. All formal results have
internal status PROVED; this means a checked supplied derivation, not formal verification or external
peer review.
The notation is local to each explicit experiment: Damb is ambient dimension, d polynomial degree,
Cdiag a diagonal-envelope coefficient, εA an approximation allowance and ηk a pilot operator bound. The
main paper’s qK = 2/K is denoted q in scalar-threshold proofs; its z-quantile notation (or zval) is used
for Gaussian critical values. Canonical complexity J and support overlap H are distinguished.

General minimax optimality and adaptation without the spectral separation are not asserted.

Assumption audit.
Gaussian isotropy gives the exact diagonal sequence and the likelihood-ratio
identities. The threshold interval alone extends whenever a valid simultaneous coordinate noise envelope
is supplied, but the direct alternative and lower bounds require their own calibration. Orthonormal
loading families produce Bessel bounds and equal noise variances; general loading results require the
additional conditions stated later in this supplement. Canonical tensor expansions avoid a spectral
gap but yield potentially large rank constants. Supplied caps and M identify the outer honesty class;
replacing them with unvalidated estimates is not covered. None of these assumptions should be described
as a routine approximation for dependent LLM representations.

G.9
Remaining problems
1. Polynomial minimax intervals for diffuse weak-signal classes at general dimensions: open. The new
optimizer establishes a working global benchmark only for binary rank-one tensors; it does not
remove the computational obstruction.
2. Sharp larger-budget rates for every loading, including every unequal weight sequence: open. L1
resolves all loadings only for M ≤τ; L2 strengthens the usable lower-bound machinery.
3. General rank-dependent and pointwise adaptation: open, with explicit impossibility results for some
oracle demands. Rate adaptation over fixed positive nested caps is already attained statistically
(A1).
4. General Tucker/TT global optimizers, validated directed-rounding arithmetic, and high-dimensional
runtime scaling: not implemented.

H.7
What these results do and do not resolve
• Efficient high-dimensional minimax computation is resolved here for r ≳√p, where G1 gives the
sharp all-budget rate. Original fixed-cap diffuse-loading weak signals remain computationally open.
• All-budget loading rates are resolved for fixed polynomial decay and the specified nonorthogonal
perturbations. General profiles remain open; the matching W2 statistical construction still uses
ideal block fits.
• General growing-cap adaptation is impossible in the G2 comparison, even between positive cap
classes. P1 gives a positive, efficiently attained pointwise rate on a strong saturated Tucker family.
It does not characterize pointwise lengths everywhere or optimal SNR thresholds.
No blanket tensor hardness theorem is inferred from low-degree results for sparse tensor PCA. Those
results concern a restricted computational model; a testing reduction for arbitrary efficient intervals still
needs an explicit, justified computational hypothesis.
