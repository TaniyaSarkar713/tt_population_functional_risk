Honest Inference for Tensor Contrasts under
Tucker and Tensor-Train Rank Bounds
Draft: September 9, 2026
Abstract
We study honest confidence intervals for prescribed orthogonal contrasts of a Gaussian tensor
mean with bounded Frobenius norm and bounded Tucker or tensor-train ranks. The actual
ranks are unknown and nonzero singular values may be arbitrarily small. Canonical tensor
expansions imply anℓ2/K bound on the relevant diagonal, which calibrates a soft-threshold
interval without rank recovery. For equal mode dimensionp, fixed orderK≥ 3, fixed rank
caps and norm boundM =τ√p, the interval has lengthO{τ (logp/p )(K−2)/(2K)}, whereτ is
the coordinate noise standard deviation. We prove that this is smaller in order than every
fixed-radius affine interval on the same class and than the expected length at zero of every
interval honest on a single-unfolding relaxation. At rank one, the diagonal image is exactly an
ℓ2/K ball. A confidence-interval specialization of classical sparse-sequence theory shows that the
displayed rate is optimal when only that diagonal is used. The optimal full-tensor rate remains
between nonmatching bounds. In 172 Gaussian simulation configurations with 2,000 replications
each, empirical coverage of the selected interval ranges from 99.75% to 100%, demonstrating
conservative calibration. The results quantify the inferential value of joint rank constraints while
distinguishing rank-cap honesty, diagonal-information optimality and unresolved adaptation to
actual rank.
Keywords:honest confidence intervals; linear functionals; Gaussian sequence model; tensor train;
Tucker rank; nonconvex parameter space.
1 Introduction
Tensor rank models encode restrictions on several unfoldings of the same parameter. For statistical
inference, these restrictions are useful only if their effect on interval length survives uncertainty
about the actual ranks. A component can be too weak to identify reliably while still belonging to
the class over which coverage is required. Consider a third-orderp×p×p mean tensorA with
∥A∥F ≤ 1and rank at most one. Fromn = p independent full tensors with standard Gaussian
errors, the sample mean has noise levelτ =p−1/2. The normalized diagonal sump−1/2∑
iAiii has a
direct interval of length proportional toτ. Can the simultaneous rank restrictions provide a smaller
interval with coverage at every admissible mean, including zero?
Existing theory treats several aspects of this question. Tensor functional inference on regular low-
rank strata uses accurate subspace estimation and suitable signal conditions [Xu et al., 2025]. Matrix
inference without exact-rank estimation is available under quantitative strength and alignment
conditions [Choi et al., 2026]. Classical work establishes nonlinear functional estimation and the
limits of honest adaptation on nonconvex parameter sets [Cai and Low, 2004b,a, Collier et al., 2017].
These precedents motivate a narrower comparison: determine whether joint tensor rank constraints

permit a length improvement that cannot be retained by intervals required to cover a larger model
defined by a single unfolding.
We study this comparison in the fully observed Gaussian mean experiment, with a known noise
variance, a supplied Frobenius bound and supplied upper rank bounds. The loading is a sum
of orthogonal product tensors. Honesty means a finite-sample lower bound on coverage over the
entire rank-cap class; it includes every lower-rank stratum and every arbitrarily small nonzero
singular value. For fixedK≥ 3, equal mode sizep and M =τ√p, our interval has lengtho(τ). By
comparison, any honest fixed-radius affine interval on the same class has length at least a constant
times τ, and any interval honest over a single-unfolding relaxation has expected length at zero at
least a constant timesτ. Matricization preserves the data. The latter comparison concerns the
parameter space over which coverage is required.
Three mathematical issues govern the argument. Uniform control at rank boundaries requires
an envelope that avoids inverses of small singular values. A lower bound for the enlarged model
must apply to arbitrary interval procedures, including those using all tensor observations. Finally,
optimality depends on which observations a procedure uses: the coordinate diagonal and the
full tensor contain different statistical information. This distinction prevents a sharp result for a
Gaussian sequence marginal from being mistaken for a sharp full-tensor result.
Canonical Tucker and TT expansions control the sum of squared component weights and hence
the ℓ2/K size of an orthogonal diagonal. This exponent is familiar from tensor factor geometry [Fan
et al., 2023]. A deterministic error envelope for classical soft thresholding [Donoho and Johnstone,
1994] then produces an honest interval. Common-target Gaussian mixtures provide the comparison
lower bounds. In the third-order example, the radius is at mostτ{8 log(2p/α)/p}1/6. At rank one,
an exact diagonal-image identity permits a specialization of sparse-sequence theory [Collier et al.,
2017]; its lower bound matches this order for diagonal procedures, while the lower bound for the full
experiment is smaller.
The paper develops three results:
1. An explicit diagonal envelope for Tucker and TT cap classes, and a finite-sample interval valid
across their lower-rank strata (Sections 4–5).
2. Two quantitative separations: from fixed-radius affine intervals under the same honesty class,
and from all intervals under a single-unfolding relaxation (Section 5).
3. A full-data lower bracket and an explicitly attributed classical diagonal-information benchmark,
which distinguish the proved comparisons from unrestricted optimality; simulations examine
coverage, conservatism and weak-rank behavior (Sections 5.3–6).

Honest adaptation and weak ranks.The confidence-interval theory of Cai and Low [2004a]
uses between-class moduli and testing to characterize adaptation, while Low [1997] gives foundational
limitations. Finite convex-union aggregation is developed by Juditsky and Nemirovski [2020]. Thus,
neither nonlinear inference nor failure of uniform adaptation at a model boundary is peculiar to
tensors. Matrix confidence sets and detection results further show that attainable rank adaptation
depends on the observation design and loss [Carpentier et al., 2018, Carpentier and Nickl, 2015].
In econometric models, Choi et al. [2026] studyYit =XitMit +Eit and construct studentized
block-average inference with a projection dimension at least the true rank. Their quantitative
strength and alignment assumptions remain relevant even though exact rank is not estimated.
Armstrong et al. [2026] consider a regression coefficient in a panel with a low-rank interactive effect,
using weights and a bias bound for uniform inference without a minimum factor strength. Zhu [2019]
establishes weak-factor and rank-adaptation obstructions in a missing-entry experiment. These are
different targets and designs from the fully observed tensor contrast considered here.
Regular matrix and tensor functional inference.For tensor regressionYi =⟨Xi,A⟩ +ϵi,
Xu et al. [2025] combine a pilot estimator with debiasing and tangent projection to infer general
linear functionals. Their regular-stratum results require rank/subspace control and signal/remainder
conditions; the principal regression theorem imposes a positive minimum unfolding signal. Its
tangent standard deviation is of the formσ∥PTAL∥F/√n. Our replacement is uniform finite-sample
coverage on a cap union, at the cost of a narrower loading, known norm/noise bounds and potentially
conservative length. No pointwise dominance over that efficient regime is asserted.
Related matrix linear-form inference, geometric inverse-problem inference and limiting distribu-
tions under rank caps are developed by Xia and Yuan [2021], Cai et al. [2016], Carpentier and Kim
[2018]. Tensor subspace and functional inference includes strong-signal results of Xia et al. [2022],
Agterberg and Zhang [2024]; Cai et al. [2023] treats tensor completion and entry/factor uncertainty.
Covariance-weighted low-rank regression inference [Ke et al., 2026] and uniform inference for weak
matrix signals [Bykhovskaya et al., 2025] provide recent neighboring results. Their observation
models and regularity conditions cannot be replaced by the Gaussian location experiment without
proof.
Geometry and finite-sample inference.Orthonormal Tucker and TT representations are
standard [De Lathauwer et al., 2000, Oseledets, 2011]. Tensor norms and Euclidean factor penalties
already contain the exponent2/K [Friedland and Lim, 2018, Fan et al., 2023]; tangent cones of
Tucker and TT varieties have also been studied [Gao et al., 2025, Kutschan, 2018]. We use canonical
expansions without estimating tangent spaces. Singular-model likelihood theory [Drton, 2009] and
universal inference [Wasserman et al., 2020] establish broader approaches to nonregular validity.
Our specific comparison concerns interval length under simultaneous rank constraints, rather than
generic existence of a valid interval.

6 Simulation study
6.1 Protocol and baselines
The simulation protocol was written after the theorem derivations and before implementation. It
uses seed 20260909, 2,000 replications per configuration, and exact marginal observations of the
diagonal coordinates needed by the procedures. Unused independent Gaussian coordinates of the
full tensor need not be generated. This does not simulate a regression design or a tensor completion
experiment.
The main grid takesK∈{ 2, 3, 4, 6}, p =n∈{ 128, 512, 2048, 8192}, M =σ = 1, and rank cap
one. Signals have the formA = au⊗K, witha∈{ 0, 0.02, 0.2, 1} and u uniform on a coordinate
support. For nonzero signals, support sizes include one,p, and a value near(a/λ)2/K, with duplicates
removed. The latter places diagonal means near the threshold. Additional configurations varyn/p
and the valid threshold multiplier, and use cap two with a second component tending to zero. There
are 175 configurations, of which three deliberately violate the iid Gaussian model.
We compareIG, I0, INL and C∗. For nonzero rank-one signals, a known-direction oracle is also
included. IfU =u⊗K were supplied, thenc⟨U,Z⟩±zτ|c| , wherec =⟨L,U⟩ , is exact forA =aU. Its
joint Gaussian noise with the diagonal is simulated exactly. This oracle has unavailable structural
information and a smaller honesty class; it is not an implementation of an estimated-subspace
method. Published strong-signal methods are compared through their assumptions rather than
advertised as uniform baselines without a matching implementation.

7 Discussion
The results show that the parameter class over which coverage is required can determine the order
of an honest interval’s length. For a prescribed orthogonal contrast, simultaneous tensor rank
restrictions yield an interval smaller than the order required by a single-unfolding relaxation. The
comparison holds at arbitrarily weak signals and does not depend on identifying the actual rank. It
also separates nonlinear procedures from every fixed-radius affine interval on the original class.
The exact diagonal reduction gives a precise connection with classical sequence inference. At
rank one, the attainable diagonal vectors form anℓ2/K ball; Proposition 1 then identifies the optimal
rate among diagonal-based intervals. The tensor-specific question that remains is the value of the
off-diagonal observations. Equation(22) leaves a polynomial gap for the full experiment, so the
proposed interval should not be described as unrestricted minimax optimal. Similarly, Theorem 4
applies to a larger honesty class, not to every algorithm that represents its data as a matrix.
Several restrictions affect practical inference. The loading must have the orthogonal product
form, the Frobenius and rank caps are supplied, and the noise standard deviation is known. The
cap-based radius may be loose for growing ranks and cannot shrink when an additional singular
value vanishes. The experiments show substantial conservatism even though length improves over

A sharp full-data theorem forK = 3and rank one is the most direct next question. It would
require either an interval exploiting off-diagonal information or a least-favorable full-tensor mixture
giving a stronger lower bound. A second question is loading geometry: characterize which contrasts
permit a joint-constraint advantage and which inherit the single-unfolding rate. Honest adaptation
to actual rank would additionally require valid coverage on the larger cap class and a characterization
of testable smaller-rank submodels. Rank consistency at separated signals alone is insufficient.
Applications require a sampling model that justifies the noise calibration and an independently
specified contrast. Repeated calibrated tensor measurements could provide such a model, but no
real-data experiment is claimed here. In particular, arrays indexed by LLM model, layer and prompt
generally have prompt, token and layer dependence, heterogeneous noise and data-derived loadings.
Simply stacking those observations does not establish the Gaussian experiment. Extending the
noise envelope and functional-selection theory would be necessary before an LLM experiment could
substantiate these inferential claims.
Reproducibility.The accompanying project contains the simulation protocol, modular Python
code, fixed seeds, 817 result rows, exact binomial uncertainty, source and result hashes, the literature
matrix and a theorem-status ledger. The original simulation outputs are reused without changing
the interval algorithm; the present revision adds the diagonal benchmark and exact hypergeometric
checks. All displayed numerical values come from saved outputs or analytic formulas.
