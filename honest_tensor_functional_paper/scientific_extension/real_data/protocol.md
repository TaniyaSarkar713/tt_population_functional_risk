# EEG analysis protocol, fixed before downloading raw observations

Recorded 2026-09-14. Data source: Begleiter (1995), EEG Database, UCI, DOI 10.24432/C5TS3D, CC BY 4.0. Use the full subject archive; do not pool train/test versions, which reuse subjects.

Primary estimand: the mean across eligible subjects of each subject's nonmatching-minus-matching S2 response, averaged over electrodes CZ and PZ and samples 64 through 127 (recorded sample index, not an asserted physiological latency). Clip individual voltage measurements at +/-100 microvolts BEFORE averaging. This defines a bounded recorded-response estimand, not an unclipped physiological effect. Do not infer a clinical or causal effect. Include both recorded groups; do not use the diagnosis label to select a result.

Form a subject tensor with axes condition (S1 obj, S2 match, S2 nomatch), 64 recorded channels, and 16 consecutive bins of 16 samples. Average valid trials equally within each subject-condition, then weight subjects equally. Reject empty, error-flagged, malformed/nonfinite, duplicate, or incomplete 64-by-256 trials. Require at least one valid trial per condition and both primary electrodes. Record every exclusion and condition count. No subject is excluded on voltage magnitude or effect size. Resolve schema-only changes explicitly in a deviation log.

Primary uncertainty: a two-sided empirical Bernstein interval (Maurer-Pontil, Theorem 4, applied to both signs), clipped to the known target range [-200,200]. Also show a Hoeffding interval and an explicitly approximate Student t interval. Assumption: independent identically distributed eligible subjects from the study sampling population. This is a working sampling model, not verified by this archive; family identifiers/selection probabilities are unavailable. Describe the empirical cohort mean separately. No population prevalence or clinical generalization.

Primary tensor demonstration: random seed 20260914, 25% of subjects for pilot, remaining subjects split equally between bias calibration and validation. On the pilot mean only, form Tucker projections at caps (1,2,2), (2,4,4), (3,8,8); select by pilot target-loading residual norm, breaking ties toward smaller cap. Project each subject's tensor onto the pilot-chosen loading and clip the resulting scalar to [-200,200]. Calibrate the signed mean residual (original minus projected) on the calibration subjects; estimate the projected mean on validation subjects. Sum the two means and their empirical Bernstein radii, allocating alpha/2 to each term. This interval targets the same population contrast without exact rank or Gaussian noise assumptions. Report the uncorrected projected interval only as a bias diagnostic. Compare to direct intervals on all subjects and on nonpilot subjects with sample sizes explicit. Do not select a result using held-out widths or significance.

Sensitivity: repeat clipping at 50 and 200 microvolts; these are different estimands and exploratory intervals, not simultaneous claims. Report clipping frequency, subject heterogeneity, condition counts, exclusions, actual archive subjects, and the full manifest/checksums. No coverage can be observed on real data. No raw trial is an independent inferential replication. A failure to obtain narrow certified intervals is a result to retain.
